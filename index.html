Shrubs

Shrubs can help transform the look of your home, offering beautiful framing and landscaping opportunities to elevate your lawn. Plant flowers like lilacs and shrubs for a homey and elegant feel. Or take a more relaxed approach with plants like juniper and bluebeard, which are low maintenance and drought-tolerant.

Different plants have different sunlight needs. Some prefer full sun, others partial, and some thrive in a mix of the two. Carefully review the plant care guide provided with each plant purchase to determine the best location within your home for your new plant. This is one of the most important tasks you must do as a new gardener.

Most plants require regular watering in order to thrive. We reccomend planting in well-draining soil to prevent roots from sitting in water for too long, which can lead to root rot. Cacti are more resilient and can last weeks to months without watering, and require flooding every so often.

Many plants thrive in regular garden soil, which generally sits at a pH of 6.5. However, some plants, like blueberries, prefer acidic soil, which has a pH of 4.5 to 5.5. If you plant acid-loving plants in regular garden soil, the decline in your plant's health will be quite noticable.

Plants grown in our nursery are raised without fertilizer or pesticides, making them fully organic. You can opt to maintain this practice at home if you so desire, or add fertilizer to attempt to increase your harvest and bring back a potentially declining plant. To this end, we also sell fertilizer and can advise you on the best choices for your chosen plants.

Misconceptions

Contrary to popular belief, more water is not always better. Yes, water is absolutely necessary for your plant to grow and photosynthesize. However, too much water can suffocate or rot your roots, and even lead to less nutrient absorption overall. How do you know if this is a problem? Look for yellow or droopy leaves.

Another common misconception is the direct opposite: that drought-tolerant plants never need water. Although drought-tolerant plants can last longer periods without water, they still require semi-regular watering until they are well-established.

More fertilizer is not always better. It is possible to 'over' care for your plants, and this is a prime example. Too much fertilizer can stunt plant growth and burn roots.
